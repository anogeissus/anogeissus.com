Free LUTs for ApolloOne

ApolloOne gives your photo a look from a .cube LUT, picked in the LUTs tab of Quick Edit. The sites below offer LUTs you can download for free.

What is a LUT?

A LUT, short for lookup table, is a small file that maps each color in a picture to a new one, so one file can give a photo a whole new look, such as a film stock or a black and white conversion. Filmmakers have used them for years, and the common .cube files work on photos too.

In ApolloOne the LUT goes on top of your other edits. Intensity sets how strong it is, and None takes it off.

Bring them into ApolloOne

  1. Download a pack and unzip it. Safari unzips downloads on its own.
  2. In ApolloOne, open Quick Edit, click the LUTs tab, and choose Import LUTs… from the menu at the top.
  3. Choose the unzipped folder. Each pack in it becomes a category.

ApolloOne copies only the .cube files and leaves the rest of the download alone. When a pack has one version for Log footage and one for Rec.709 or "standard", use the Rec.709 one. Your photos are not Log.

Included with ApolloOne

ApolloOne comes with 18 LUTs to start with, in the Starter category: film looks, black and white, infrared and more, by PictureFX by Marc.

For Log video

Camera makers offer free LUTs that turn their own Log footage into a normal picture. Use the one for your camera and the Log profile you shot in: F-Log2 footage needs the F-Log2 LUT. The looks above are made for Rec.709, so they don't go on Log footage directly.

  • Fujifilm F-Log, F-Log2, F-Log2 C, with film simulations such as ETERNA
  • Sony S-Log3, S-Log2
  • Panasonic LUMIX V-Log
  • Canon Canon Log 2, Canon Log 3
  • Nikon N-Log, with four looks by RED
  • DJI D-Log, D-Log M
  • Leica L-Log
  • OM SYSTEM OM-Log400
  • GoPro GP-Log
  • Insta360 I-Log
  • Apple Apple Log

Sony, Canon, Nikon and OM SYSTEM ask you to accept their license before the download. Apple's needs an Apple Account.
