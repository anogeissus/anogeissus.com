ApolloOne

The fastest image viewer on the planet — Now supercharged.

Version 5 adds tabs and windows, List View, Stacks, Focus Score, AI Rating and AI Keywords. The biggest of them form the new Max Edition, free to try for 14 days.

WIRED's Best Image Viewer two years running, now with full HDR support. Browse at up to 32 fps on M1 Ultra with 64-bit precision and 10-bit Liquid Retina display support — every image displayed at full resolution, not a scaled-down preview. Non-destructive Quick Edit with InstaBake™ for instant JPEG output via AirDrop or drag-and-drop. Full EXIF and IPTC metadata editing, GPS mapping with Google Street View, autofocus point display, batch renaming and conversion, split-view comparison, and video playback with frame-by-frame scrubbing — all without importing a single file. Supports RAW, JPEG, HEIF, TIFF, PNG, and 40+ camera formats through the RawBridge™ engine, in 11 languages.

HDR Display & Export

See your RAW files with full dynamic range on HDR-capable Macs* — 10-bit color, EDR-aware rendering, and a one-click HDR toggle in the toolbar. Filmstrip thumbnails render in HDR too, and a dual SDR + HDR histogram shows how much of your image pushes into the HDR highlight range. Export full HDR to AVIF or JPEG XL in PQ colorspace, gain-map JPEG and HEIC that stay viewable on SDR displays, or scene-linear OpenEXR. SDR exports are color-managed too: keep the original color space, or convert to sRGB, Display P3, or Adobe RGB (1998). Supports HDR RAW, Apple gain-map HEIC/JPEG, PQ-encoded AVIF, JPEG XL, and OpenEXR.

Your Exports Are Four Stops Behind Your Camera — why there are as much as four stops of highlight information above SDR white in every RAW file, and what it takes to get them all the way to someone else's screen.

HDR Visualizer

See exactly where your highlights push past SDR white. Four false-color bands — cyan at +1 EV, blue at +2 EV, purple at +3 EV, and magenta at +4 EV — overlay the image to show how bright each highlight actually is. Hover over any band in the histogram to isolate just that zone on the image.

Hover any pixel to read its per-channel value in the inspector's RGB patches — 0–255 for SDR, +X.X EV for channels that extend into HDR. Exact headroom, per channel, to a tenth of a stop.

Quick Edit

Non-destructive adjustments right inside the viewer. Tweak exposure, highlights, shadows, temperature, tint, contrast, saturation, vibrance, and unsharp mask — then export instantly with InstaBake™ via AirDrop or drag-and-drop. No round-trip to another app required.

ML Processing

Apple's machine learning RAW decoder. Turn on RAW > Use ML Processing and every supported RAW file is decoded with machine learning noise reduction; X-Trans files get a better demosaic as well. It applies to the image on screen and to everything you export, batch exports included. The title bar shows +ML while a machine learning render is on screen, and a checkbox in Quick Edit keeps any single image on the standard decoder.

Apple's decoder covers 51 camera models today, and macOS updates add more, which ApolloOne picks up on its own. A file it does not cover is rendered normally. The machine learning decode takes longer than the standard one, so thumbnails and the slideshow stay on the standard decoder. Requires macOS 27 or later.

Hierarchical Keywords

A keyword tree with parents and synonyms, written the way Lightroom and Photo Mechanic read it. Keep your vocabulary as Animal > Bird > Osprey, drag a branch to rearrange it, give a keyword its synonyms, and import, merge or export a whole dictionary. Applying a keyword writes the entire branch into the file, so a search for Bird finds the ospreys too.

Starter vocabularies are included in all 11 languages, ready to build on.

Tagging, Rating & Keywords

Organize your library the way you think. Add titles, descriptions, keywords, locations, and copyright — all written as XMP-standard metadata directly into the file. 5-star rate and color-label images for quick culling, with support for both XMP labels and macOS Finder color tags — so your tags stay visible in Finder too. Copy and paste metadata across files in batch.

Version 5 adds a Custom tab to the Edit Metadata panel with the full IPTC Core and IPTC Extension catalog, grouped into Content, Status, Creator, Location, Rights and Extension — City, State/Province, Country, Credit Line, Source, Instructions, Rights Usage Terms, Web Statement of Rights, Licensor URL, Digital Source Type, Event and the creator's contact block. Customize… hides the fields you never use so the form stays short, and every field loads, saves, copies and pastes across a whole selection the way the basic ones do. Web Statement of Rights and Licensor URL are the two fields Google Images reads for its Licensable badge.

Stacks

Group a burst into one pile. Forty frames collapse to a single thumbnail badged ×40, carrying the rating and tags of its key photo and a name if you give it one. Stack a selection, drag thumbnails onto a pile to add them, and open the pile when you want to look inside.

Auto-Stack groups a whole folder by the time between frames — the grid re-piles as you drag the slider, so you see the grouping before you commit to it. Bracketed sets, panorama sequences and continuous shooting stop filling the grid.

Focus Score

Grade a folder on how sharply the camera focused. Every still image is measured at the autofocus points your camera recorded, at full resolution, and gets a number from 1 to 100. The scores show on the thumbnails, in the filmstrip and in their own List View column, and the Filter panel collects them into eight score bands, each with a count, so the frames that missed can be pulled out in one click. Processing runs in the background while you keep browsing.

AI Keywords

Keywords proposed for every photo: the subject, the species of an animal or plant by its common name, a recognizable place or landmark, a dish, and up to ten keywords a photographer would file the photo under. A preset tells the model what kind of shoot it is looking at — wildlife, birds, plants, landscape, wedding, people, food — without telling it what it must find, so a house in a folder of birds is still a house.

Proposals arrive as columns in List View next to the keywords the file carries today, and nothing is written until you apply them. Answers come back in the language of the vocabulary you are working in, and photos with GPS give the model the place, which helps it tell look-alike species apart.

The analysis runs on Apple's Private Cloud Compute, the same server-side Apple Intelligence macOS itself uses. A 1024-pixel copy of each photo is sent, Apple's servers answer and keep nothing, and the photos are not used for training. No third-party account, API key or subscription.

AI Rating

Every photo rated 1 to 5 on its merit as a picture: the light, the color, the composition, how clearly the subject reads, the moment. A 5 is worth printing, a 1 has nothing to recommend it. Sharpness never lowers a rating — a portrait shot wide open is soft on purpose — while a Focus Score of 90 or more lifts a 4 to 5 stars, for a photo that is excellent and technically excellent too. Up to three short reasons are kept with every photo: warm light, strong color, busy background. 300 photos are rated in about three minutes.

Like AI Keywords, it runs on Apple's Private Cloud Compute: no third-party account, API key or subscription.

Tabs & Windows

Browse several shoots at the same time. Each tab keeps its own folder, selection, filters, sort order and display mode, so you can leave one shoot open, go looking through another, and switch back without waiting for a folder to load again. ⌘T adds a tab, ⌘N opens a second window, and dragging a tab out of the row makes it a window of its own.

Contact Sheet & Histogram

Browse hundreds of images in a thumbnail grid with RAW+JPEG pair badges and GPS indicators. Select any image to see full file details, camera settings, and a live RGB histogram — all without leaving the grid.

List View

The Contact Sheet as a sortable table. One row per image, with a thumbnail and the columns you would otherwise open the Inspector to read — filename, date, ISO, aperture, shutter, exposure compensation, focal length, lens and tags. Sort the whole folder by any of them to pick between frames on their settings rather than one at a time.

Add the Keywords column to type keywords straight into a row, with the same autocomplete as the metadata panel, and the Focus Score column to bring the sharpest frames to the top. Choose which columns you want from the heading menu.

Autofocus Point Display

See exactly where your camera focused. ApolloOne reads AF point data embedded in your RAW and JPEG files and overlays them directly on the image — so you can instantly verify focus accuracy without zooming in. It also displays faces detected by the camera from embedded metadata. A feature no other viewer offers.

Side-by-Side Comparison

Compare two images simultaneously with split view and synchronized zoom. Perfect for picking the best shot from a burst, comparing RAW vs JPEG, or evaluating different compositions — with full EXIF and map context alongside.

Deep EXIF & Metadata

Every detail your camera records, at a glance. A searchable metadata inspector shows EXIF, GPS, lens info, and more — alongside a full Places browser for navigating your photo library by folder or external drive. Double-click any editable field to change it on the fly — no separate dialog needed.

GPS & Built-in Maps

Every geotagged photo shows its location on an embedded map with reverse-geocoded place names, coordinates, and altitude. Your choice of Apple Maps, with Standard, Hybrid, and Satellite views, or Google Maps, with Standard, Hybrid, and Street View. On either map, drag the marker to a new spot to update the photo's GPS data, or search for any location directly from the map.

Google Street View

Go beyond the pin. Drop into Google Street View right from the sidebar to see the actual surroundings where you took the photo — no browser needed. Perfect for revisiting a location or scouting the area around a shoot.

Customizable Status Bar

The strip along the bottom of the window reads out the shot: where you are in the folder, the image size, the date, the place the photo was taken, ISO, aperture, shutter, exposure compensation, focal length, color model and camera. It is the same readout in the viewer, the Contact Sheet and List View.

View > Customize Status Bar, or a right-click on the bar, raises a sheet of every field the bar is not showing. Each pill carries the current image's own value, so you can see what a field holds before you add it. Drag a pill into the bar to add it, drag a field out to remove it, and drag a divider to change a field's width. The sheet offers lens, focal length in 35mm, filename, file type, file size, white balance, metering mode, flash, software, color profile, orientation and the creation and modification dates, plus two spacers. Changes save as you make them and apply to every tab and window.

AppleScript Automation

ApolloOne is scriptable. A script reads and writes the metadata of the images you selected, the whole selection at once, through the same writer the Edit Metadata panel uses. Scripts run from Script Editor, from the Shortcuts app's Run AppleScript action, from osascript in Terminal, and from any app that sends Apple events.

Every image walks the active window in the order you see it, with any filter applied. Color label, rating and pick are cheap to read, so a script can sift thousands of files in one pass and act on the culling you already did by hand.

Free scripts that keyword your photos with Claude AI, then fill in titles and locations, are on the Keyword with Claude AI page.

tell application "ApolloOne"
    open "/Volumes/Photos/2026/Wedding"
    repeat with img in (every image whose rating ≥ 4 and pick is true)
        set keywords of img to {"+Selects"}
    end repeat
end tell
