ApolloOne

The fastest image viewer on the planet — Now supercharged.

Version 5 adds tabs and windows, List View, Stacks, Focus Score, AI Rating and AI Keywords. The biggest of them form the new Max Edition, free to try for 14 days.

WIRED's Best Image Viewer two years running, now with full HDR support. Browse at up to 55 fps on M1 Ultra with 64-bit precision and 10-bit Liquid Retina display support — every image displayed at full resolution, not a scaled-down preview. Non-destructive Quick Edit with InstaBake™ for instant JPEG output via AirDrop or drag-and-drop. Full EXIF and IPTC metadata editing, GPS mapping with Google Street View, autofocus point display, batch renaming and conversion, split-view comparison, and video playback with frame-by-frame scrubbing — all without importing a single file. Supports RAW, JPEG, HEIF, TIFF, PNG, and 40+ camera formats through the RawBridge™ engine, in 11 languages.

Immersive Image View

An immersive image view, with an optional Liquid Glass interface: the toolbar and the status bar float on glass over the photo, the same glass macOS uses for its own toolbars. Click the photo to zoom in, or press and hold to look closer. Liquid Glass requires macOS 26 Tahoe or later.

Tab Overview

In Liquid Glass, the Tabs button lays out every tab of the window with the photo it is showing. Click one to switch to it, close the ones you are done with, or open a new one. A video shows the frame where you paused it, and a split view shows both of its photos.

HDR Display & Export

See RAW files with their full dynamic range on HDR-capable Macs*, thumbnails included, with an SDR + HDR histogram. Export HDR to AVIF, JPEG XL, gain-map JPEG and HEIC, or OpenEXR, and color-managed SDR to sRGB, Display P3 or Adobe RGB.

Your Exports Are Four Stops Behind Your Camera — the highlights above SDR white in every RAW file, and how to get them to someone else's screen.

HDR Visualizer

Four false-color bands show how far each highlight goes past SDR white: cyan at +1 EV, blue at +2, purple at +3 and magenta at +4. Hover a pixel to read each channel's value, to a tenth of a stop in HDR.

Quick Edit

Non-destructive adjustments in the viewer: exposure, highlights, shadows, temperature, tint, contrast, saturation, vibrance and unsharp mask. Export the result straight away with InstaBake™, by AirDrop or drag-and-drop.

ML Processing

Apple's machine learning RAW decoder brings machine learning noise reduction and a better demosaic to every supported camera, on screen and in every export. Fujifilm X-Trans files improve the most. It covers 51 camera models today, and macOS updates add more. Requires macOS 27 or later.

Cameras Supported by ML Processing. Below: the standard decode on the left, the ML-processed export on the right, both at 200%.

Hierarchical Keywords

A keyword tree with parents and synonyms, in the format Lightroom and Photo Mechanic read. Applying Animal > Bird > Osprey writes the whole branch, so a search for Bird finds the ospreys too. Starter vocabularies come in all 11 languages.

Code Replacements expand short codes into full text when the metadata is saved: type =ny= in a caption and it is saved as New York City, New York, USA. They work in every metadata field, in keywords and in rename rules, together with variables such as {filename} and the capture date. Code replacement files from Photo Mechanic work as they are.

Tagging, Rating & Keywords

Titles, descriptions, keywords, locations and copyright, written into the file as XMP, plus star ratings, color labels and Finder tags. The Custom tab adds the full IPTC Core and Extension set, including the two fields Google Images reads for its Licensable badge.

Stacks

A burst of forty frames collapses into one thumbnail badged ×40, carrying the key photo's rating and tags. Auto-Stack groups a whole folder by the time between frames and re-piles the grid as you drag its slider.

Focus Score

Every photo scored 1 to 100 on how sharp it is at the autofocus points your camera recorded, at full resolution, in the background. The Filter panel sorts the scores into bands, so the frames that missed can be pulled out in one click.

How the ApolloOne Focus Score Works

AI Keywords

Keywords proposed for every photo: the subject, the species by its common name, a landmark, a dish. Nothing is written until you apply them. It runs on Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which keeps nothing. No third-party account, API key or subscription.

AI Rating

Every photo rated 1 to 5 on its merit as a picture (light, color, composition, the moment), with up to three short reasons. Sharpness never lowers a rating. 300 photos take about three minutes, on Apple's Private Cloud Compute.

Tabs & Windows

Browse several shoots at once. Each tab keeps its own folder, selection, filters, sort order and display mode, and a tab dragged out of the row becomes a window of its own.

Contact Sheet & Histogram

Hundreds of images in a thumbnail grid with RAW+JPEG pair badges and GPS indicators, and a live RGB histogram and file details for the one you select.

List View

The Contact Sheet as a sortable table: one row per image with filename, date, ISO, aperture, shutter, exposure compensation, focal length, lens and tags. Add the Keywords column to type keywords into a row, or the Focus Score column to bring the sharpest frames to the top.

Autofocus Point Display

See where your camera focused. ApolloOne overlays the AF points recorded in RAW and JPEG files, and the faces the camera detected, so you can check focus without zooming in.

Side-by-Side Comparison

Two images side by side with synchronized zoom, to pick the best frame of a burst or compare RAW with JPEG.

Deep EXIF & Metadata

Every detail your camera records, in a searchable inspector: EXIF, GPS, lens and more. Double-click an editable field to change it in place.

GPS & Built-in Maps

Geotagged photos show on a map with the place name, coordinates and altitude, on Apple Maps or Google Maps. Drag the marker to correct a photo's location.

Google Street View

See the surroundings where you took the photo in Google Street View, right from the sidebar.

Customizable Status Bar

The bar along the bottom reads out the shot: position in the folder, image size, date, place, ISO, aperture, shutter and more. Drag fields in or out to choose what it shows; each one previews the current image's value before you add it.

AppleScript Automation

Scripts run from Script Editor, the Shortcuts app or osascript, and read and write the metadata of the whole selection at once. They can read color labels, ratings and picks too, to act on the culling you already did.

Free scripts that keyword your photos with Claude AI are on the Keyword with Claude AI page.

tell application "ApolloOne"
    open "/Volumes/Photos/2026/Wedding"
    repeat with img in (every image whose rating ≥ 4 and pick is true)
        set keywords of img to {"+Selects"}
    end repeat
end tell

Ready to try ApolloOne?

Free to download. 14-day free trial of the Max Edition. Subscription and one-time purchase perpetual licenses available.

After the trial, ApolloOne continues to work as a free edition — no subscription required, no lockout. Upgrade whenever you're ready.

Requires macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

Liquid Glass with its tab overview, Stacks, Focus Score, AI Rating, AI Keywords, ML Processing, Code Replacements, the customizable status bar and AppleScript automation require the Max Edition; List View, tabs and windows require the Standard Edition. The Keywords Manager is in every edition — the tree, the vocabularies, import, merge and export; the Max Edition is what writes a whole keyword branch, parents and synonyms included, into the file. AI Rating and AI Keywords also require macOS 27 or later on a Mac with Apple silicon, with Apple Intelligence turned on and the Mac signed in to iCloud, and Apple sets a daily limit per iCloud account.

* HDR display requires an EDR-capable screen: a MacBook Pro with Liquid Retina XDR (14″ or 16″, 2021 or later), or any Mac paired with an Apple Studio Display, Apple Studio Display XDR, 30″ Apple Pro Display XDR, or a compatible third-party HDR display. Peak brightness varies: up to 600 nits on Studio Display, 1,600 nits on Pro Display XDR, and 2,000 nits on Studio Display XDR. SDR-only Macs display HDR images in SDR with automatic tone-mapping.
